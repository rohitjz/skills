# Positioning output template

Replace the prompts below with findings. Preserve the nine sections and their
order. Use evidence IDs to keep the recommendation auditable.

## 1. Executive verdict

- Strongest opportunity and its audience payoff.
- Why this idea can credibly own that position.
- Recommended next move and the main condition or uncertainty.

If none of the territories is defensible, say so here. Distinguish a provisional
test direction from a launch recommendation.

## 2. Professional diagnosis

Summarize the idea, audience, goal, market, and constraints.
Label assumptions. Cover what an experienced strategist investigates beyond a
brainstorm, the raw material examined, the methods selected and why, and the
observable evidence that would demonstrate success.

## 4. Three positioning territories

For each of three different directions, provide:

- Strategic insight, with evidence or hypothesis status.
- Conventional pattern and actual examples when available.
- Distinctive contrast.
- Psychological mechanism, supporting evidence, and limits.
- Audience payoff.
- Credibility anchor.
- Execution implications and an illustrative concept.
- Risk.

Use category misfit, category reframe, and belief challenger as starting lenses.
Explain any replacement lens.

## 6. Winning positioning system

- Positioning statement: audience, need, distinctive frame, outcome, reason to
  believe, conventional alternative, and meaningful difference.
- Signature point of view: our belief, category belief, audience usefulness,
  and supporting proof.
- Content identity: recurring formats, hooks, storytelling patterns, visual or
  verbal cues, topics to avoid, and how to sustain variety.
- Repeatable creative rule specific to this idea.

When no direction clears the checks, label this section a provisional test system.

## 8. Validation experiment

Define the hypothesis, audience, recruitment or distribution, assignment,
control, differentiated concept, constants, primary metric and denominator,
measurement window, guardrails, practical decision criteria, sample-size or
uncertainty considerations, and stopping rule.

inputs rather than inventing benchmarks or statistical confidence.

## 9. Uncertainties

List the unknowns most likely to change the decision, assumptions that matter,
and evidence that would change the recommendation. End with the next concrete
evidence-gathering action.
